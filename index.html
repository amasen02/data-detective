Data Detective Case File #15

Can you trust this claim? A 10–15 minute investigation into how numbers get framed — for curious minds ~15 and up.

Detective's Briefing

A news alert flashes on your screen:

“TEEN SCREEN TIME EXPLODES — city teens' daily screen hours DOUBLED in eight weeks, new survey shows.”

— The Daily Byte (fictional newspaper), Week 8 of the Metropolis Youth Study (fictional study)

Your job is not to decide "true or false" in one bite. Real detectives check how the number was made: how the picture was drawn, who was asked, and what else moved alongside it. You'll practice three moves today:

  • The Graph Gambit — how axis choices change the look of the same numbers.
  • Sampling Sleuth — why who you ask can bend an answer more than how many you ask.
  • Cause or Coincidence? — two things rising together, and the sneaky third thing behind them.

Everything here is fictional and synthetic — invented people, invented city, invented study. Any resemblance to real debates is the point, not the evidence.

How this works: progress saves automatically in this browser; nothing leaves your device, no account needed.

Prerequisites: can read a simple table and a bar chart. Time: ~12 minutes. If anything looks off, every activity has a Hint button and unlimited retries — mistakes are evidence too.
